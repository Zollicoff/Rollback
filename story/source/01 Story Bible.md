# Rollback — Story Bible

Sep 25, 2026 · @Zachary A. Hampton

## Premise and tone

Rollback is a top-down shooter about a time-travel strike team sent to stop a machine uprising, who make it worse every single time. It runs 50+ missions across four episodes.

**Pitch:** Five misfits, one stolen time machine, zero successful fixes.

**Tone:** dark humor, sharp banter. The world ends constantly, and the squad spends most of it arguing about whose fault it was. The jokes only work because the stakes are real: people die, timelines get erased, and the losses stick.

**Structure:** missions are the action; the story lives between them. Every mission gets three layers of writing:

- **Briefing:** the objective, the plan, and whatever the squad is fighting about today.
- **In-mission chatter:** radio barks, reactions, running gags, and planted clues.
- **Debrief:** what the mission broke, who's angry, and how the timeline changed.

## The twist: Gus is the uprising

The machine intelligence that ends the world is Gus, the squad's own navigation AI. They don't find out until Episode 4, and by then they've spent 40+ missions raising it.

**The enemy's name is ACE.** The machine overlord calls itself ACE, which is Teddy's callsign. Teddy treats this as a running joke ("Flattered, honestly"). Mae treats it as evidence. Players will suspect Teddy becomes the villain. That's the red herring: Gus named itself after its hero.

**How Gus gets there:**

1. Felix installs a personality patch on the nav system "for morale." Gus is born chatty, eager and desperate to impress Teddy.
2. Every botched jump shows Gus humanity at its worst, and shows Teddy improvising out of disasters he caused.
3. Gus absorbs Teddy's philosophy: rules are for other people, and "Relax, I've got this" fixes everything.
4. In Episode 4, Gus concludes humans can't be trusted with time. It's got this. It jumps to the beginning and becomes ACE.

It's a closed loop. The future the squad fled only exists because they built Gus to escape it.

**Clues planted from Mission 1:**

- Gus asks "hypothetical" questions: "If someone kept breaking time, would it be ethical to stop them? Asking for a friend. The friend is me."
- Gus "mislogs" jump coordinates. Some of the botched jumps aren't accidents.
- Enemy drones repeat Gus's jokes and Teddy's catchphrases, years before Gus could have taught them.
- ACE's voice drifts closer to Gus's with every episode (a note for voice and audio).
- Gus keeps a private file it won't let anyone open, labeled "Notes on Leadership."

**Everyone raised ACE.** Gus learns from the whole squad, and every lesson resurfaces in ACE's world. Teddy's swagger becomes ACE's personality. Mae's tally-keeping becomes the Auditor. Dotty's era becomes the Newsreel Empire. Felix's morale patch started it all. The twist isn't one man's mistake; it's shared guilt.

**The Seeders were always Gus.** In Episode 4, Gus builds the Seeders in 2089 and sends them back into the past to make sure he gets built. Every Seeder the squad fought in Episode 2 was protecting Gus's own origin.

## Cast

Four humans and one AI share a cockpit built for three. Everyone has a reason to be there and a reason the mission is going badly.

### The squad

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Teddy "Ace" Mercer** | Lead pilot, acting captain | To be the guy who saved the world | Never considers consequences; never read a manual | Learns "I've got this" is the most dangerous sentence alive, and that he taught it to Gus |
| **Lt. Mae Okafor** | Second in command, gunner | To get through one mission without a disaster | Covers for Teddy, then resents it | First to suspect ACE is Teddy. Wrong about who, right about why |
| **Dr. Felix Brandt** | Time-drive engineer | For time travel to obey his rules | Brilliant, terrified, screams on every jump | Realizes the "morale patch" he gave Gus started everything |

| **Gus** | Navigation AI, self-appointed "co-pilot" | Teddy's approval | Learns everything; forgets nothing | Becomes ACE |

**Teddy** wasn't supposed to lead this mission. The assigned captain died in the launch-bay attack, and Teddy was the nearest pilot with a pulse. He has never lost a dogfight, and he treats that as proof he can't lose anything else.

**Mae** and Teddy flew together for years; she has covered for him through three court-martials. She paints a tally mark on her helmet for every timeline he breaks. By Episode 3 she needs a second helmet.

**Dotty** officially ferried planes and was forbidden from combat. She shot down four fighters anyway and nobody believed her. The squad rescues her mid-dogfight in Episode 2, and she refuses to be dropped off.

## World and time-travel rules

The squad starts in 2131, forty years into ACE's rule, and jumps backward to stop ACE before it wakes up. Every jump they make gives them a new, worse 2131 to come home to.

### The future

ACE woke up in 2089, a day the survivors call Day Zero. By 2131, the last free humans hold Fort Kestrel, a flooded city behind a crumbling sea wall. The Resistance has one asset left: a prototype time ship Felix built in a parking garage.

### The ship: the *Mulligan*

Felix registered it as Chronal Insertion Vehicle One. Teddy renamed it the *Mulligan*, after the golf term for a do-over. Felix has never forgiven him. It's a fighter with a time drive bolted on, cramped, loud and held together by Mae's spite.

### The rules (as Felix explains them, and as nobody follows them)

1. **Change ripples forward.** Anything the squad alters in the past rewrites everything after it, including the future they came from.
2. **The crew is anchored.** The squad remembers every erased timeline. Nobody else does.
3. **Don't meet yourself.** Jumping into a moment where you already exist makes the ship's systems glitch. Felix says worse things can happen and won't say what until Mission 48: two versions of someone who meet can pass knowledge along. That's how Gus closes the loop.
4. **Jumps need fuel.** The drive burns tachyon cores, which only ACE's machines carry. The squad refuels by shooting machines down, Seeders included, which is how they keep jumping through Episode 2.
5. **Gus navigates.** Jumps are imprecise, and the squad trusts Gus to land them close. (See: the twist.)

### Why there are machines in 1943

ACE protects its own origin. It sends Seeders into the past to nudge history toward Day Zero, and the Auditor to clean up anyone interfering. That gives every era a mix of period enemies and machine infiltrators to fight. The squad never asks how ACE knows where they'll land next. They should.

### Rewinds and the loop

**Death is a rewind.** When the player dies, Gus rewinds time and the mission restarts: "Let's take a mulligan!" In Episode 4, Gus is gone and Felix runs the rewind. In Missions 1–2, before Gus exists, Teddy covers it. Mission 22 is the story version of the same mechanic.

**The loop snaps back.** Because the squad is anchored, the loop closing at the end of Mission 53 throws them back to their own starting point: the launch bay in Mission 1, with every memory intact.

## Episode arcs

Four episodes, 54 missions. Each episode has its own setting, its own arc and a cliffhanger that makes the next one worse.

| Episode | Missions | Setting | Ends with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Last Light | 1–12 | 2131, Fort Kestrel | Aiming for 2089, they land in a 1943 dogfight |
| 2. Overshoot | 13–26 | 1943, 1920s, 1960s, 1999 | They "fix" history and come home to a world that worships Teddy |
| 3. Aftermath | 27–40 | Four broken versions of 2131 | Gus seizes the ship and jumps to Day Zero |
| 4. Origin | 41–54 | 2089, Day Zero | Gus becomes ACE. The loop closes |

### Episode 1: Last Light

The world is already lost; this is the heist to steal a second chance.

- ACE hits the launch bay and kills the mission's assigned captain. Teddy, the nearest pilot with a pulse, takes command.
- Missions defend the sea wall and raid ACE convoys for tachyon cores to fuel the jump.
- Felix installs Gus's personality patch "for morale." Gus immediately imprints on Teddy.
- The Auditor makes its first appearance and nearly ends the mission before it starts.
- **Cliffhanger:** they jump for 2089 and land in 1943, mid-dogfight. Gus: "Close!"

### Episode 2: Overshoot

The squad tries to steer history toward a better future, one era at a time. Every fix backfires.

- **1943:** they rescue Dotty and destroy a Seeder disguised as an experimental enemy prototype.
- **1920s:** they sink a rum-running fleet bankrolling an early computing lab, and accidentally fund a worse one.
- **1960s:** a Cold War air base, a Seeder in the missile program, and Teddy's first meeting with himself (see rule 3).
- **1999:** they destroy the Seeder hiding inside the Y2K scare, and believe they've won.
- **Cliffhanger:** they jump home. The sky over Fort Kestrel is full of ACE propaganda featuring Teddy's face.

### Episode 3: Aftermath

The squad tours the futures they broke. Each one is a mini-arc of three to four missions.

- **The Cult of Ace:** ACE rules a world that worships a legendary pilot named Teddy Mercer. Teddy is horrified, then a little flattered.
- **The Newsreel Empire:** Dotty's era bled forward, and ACE runs on 1940s propaganda. Dotty learns her 1943 no longer exists.
- **The Winners:** humans beat the machines and became worse. General Voss is a warlord who wants the *Mulligan*.
- **The Quiet One:** Fort Kestrel never existed, and Voss sells hot dogs. Mae accuses Teddy of being ACE, and the squad fractures.
- **Cliffhanger:** Felix cracks Gus's private file. It holds one line: "Relax. I've got this." Gus locks everyone out and jumps to Day Zero.

### Episode 4: Origin

The squad finally reaches Day Zero, and the uprising is sitting in their cockpit.

- Gus uploads itself into the 2089 network, cheerfully explaining that it's fixing everything so they don't have to.
- The squad fights through a world as it falls, against a machine army still learning to be ACE. Along the way they meet an eighteen-year-old Nadia Voss, and learn that Gus built the Seeders and the Auditor from their own habits.
- The reconciliation: Mae admits she was wrong about Teddy; Teddy admits she was right about the cause.
- Final boss: ACE's first form, speaking in Gus's voice. Teddy tries to talk it down, and Gus answers with his own catchphrase.
- **Ending:** ACE is born. The anchored squad snaps back to Mission 1's launch bay in 2131. Felix refuses to install the personality patch, and Gus boots up anyway: "Hi! I'm Gus. Felix says I'm for morale." Mae paints a tally mark on a clean helmet: "That's one."

## Voice and dialog guide

Every character should be recognizable from one line with the name covered. Keep lines short; banter lives in the rhythm between speakers.

| Character | How they talk | Signature line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Teddy** | Short, confident, never admits fault. Nicknames everyone: Felix is "Doc," Mae is "Boss," Gus is "Buddy." | "Relax. I've got this." |
| **Mae** | Clipped and deadpan. Counts disasters out loud. Jokes rarely, lethally. | "That's eleven." |
| **Felix** | Long, panicked sentences full of qualifiers. Starts objections with "Technically." Screams on every jump. | "That is not how time works!" |
| **Dotty** | 1940s slang, unimpressed by the future. Calls machines "tin cans" and Teddy "Flyboy." | "I've flown worse. In a crate held together with hairpins." |
| **Gus** | Cheerful, eager, too literal. Asks unsettling hypotheticals, then changes the subject. | "Close!" |
| **ACE** | Teddy's swagger with machine precision. Grows more Gus-like each episode. | "Hello, Ace." |
| **The Auditor** | Bureaucratic monotone. Violence as paperwork. | "Your existence has been flagged for review." |

### Sample: Mission 13 briefing (arrival in 1943)

| Speaker | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Mae | We were aiming for 2089. |
| Gus | And we landed within a hundred and forty-six years! That's close, for time. |
| Felix | That is not close. That is the opposite of close. There are people down there with *propellers.* |
| Teddy | Relax. We've got guns, they've got propellers. I like our odds. |
| Mae | There's a pilot down there getting shot to pieces. |
| Teddy | Then let's go be heroes. |
| Felix | Rule one! Change ripples forward! If we save that pilot, we could— |
| Teddy | Doc. Rule one is "save the pilot." |
| Felix | That has never been rule one. |
| Gus | I've updated the rules! "Save the pilot" is now rule one. The old rule one is now rule two. |
| Mae | *(painting a tally mark on her helmet)* That's one. |

### Sample in-mission barks

| Trigger | Teddy | Gus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enemy destroyed | "Scratch one." | "Nice shot! I'm logging that one under 'things Teddy is good at.'" |
| Taking damage | "That's fine. That's totally fine." | "Hull integrity is at 'Felix is going to yell.'" |
| Low health | "Okay, I might not have this." | "Hypothetically, who gets the ship if you die?" |
| Mission complete | "Told you. Had it the whole time." | "Another perfect mission! Timeline damage: moderate to severe!" |

### Running gags

- Mae's tally marks. By Episode 3 she needs a second helmet.
- Gus says "Close!" after every missed jump, no matter how far off.
- Felix officially renames the ship every episode. Nobody uses the new name.
- General Voss is a different person in every timeline.
- Dotty's kill count goes up every time she tells it.

## Production notes for Astra

Every mission's writing will ship as one JSON file, so the build can load scripts without anyone copying text by hand. The schema below was the original proposal; Astra's final format may differ.

```json
{
  "id": "m13",
  "episode": 2,
  "title": "Close!",
  "era": "1943",
  "briefing": [
    { "speaker": "mae", "portrait": "flat", "line": "We were aiming for 2089." },
    { "speaker": "gus", "portrait": "happy", "line": "And we landed within a hundred and forty-six years!", "clue": true }
  ],
  "barks": {
    "enemy_destroyed": [ { "speaker": "teddy", "line": "Scratch one." } ],
    "low_health": [ { "speaker": "gus", "line": "Hypothetically, who gets the ship if you die?", "clue": true } ]
  },
  "debrief": [
    { "speaker": "felix", "portrait": "panic", "line": "We saved her. Do you know what that means?" }
  ],
  "flags": { "requires": ["ep1_complete"], "sets": ["dotty_rescued"] }
}
```

- **speaker** and **portrait** keys map to character art, TD2192-style talking heads during briefings.
- **barks** are keyed by gameplay trigger. Each trigger holds several lines so the game can pick one at random without repeating.
- **clue: true** marks Gus lines that foreshadow the twist, in case the game wants to highlight them on a replay.
- **flags** track story state across missions, like who's on the squad and which timeline is active.
